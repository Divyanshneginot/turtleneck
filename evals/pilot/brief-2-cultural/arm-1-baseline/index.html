Curatorial Proposition

The Geology of Attention: Sound in Liquid Stone

Architecture has historically been constructed to enclose and insulate against natural forces. Archipelago 2027 turns this premise inside out: we treat the volcanic caldera not as an empty site, but as an active transducer.

When magma cools into cellular pumice and basalt, it captures geological time in porous cavities. These cavities possess unique acoustic reverberation constants (RT60) that respond to atmospheric wind shear, pelagic water pressure, and low-frequency tectonic friction. The pavilions built here do not speak over the earth; they listen to it.

For eighteen days, international practitioners will occupy five distinct geological formations: from the sulfur fumaroles of Nea Kameni to the chalk cliffs of Aspronisi and the submerged hydrothermal vents of Kolumbo Seamount. Each work is constructed with bio-compatible volcanic cast aggregates, salt lattices, and tuned acoustic chambers that will weather, erode, and dissolve back into the sea.

"We are not bringing sound to silent islands. The volcanic arc has been singing at frequencies below human hearing for two million years. Our architecture is simply a tuning peg."

Visitors will travel between islands exclusively via solar-electric silent catamarans, passing through marine acoustic sanctuaries where underwater hydrophone hydro-arrays broadcast the living pulse of the subterranean mantle.

Nea Kameni

The Caldera Hearth • Basalt Amphitheaters

Formed through historical lava outflows between 1570 and 1950, Nea Kameni is an active volcanic dome characterized by steaming sulfur fumaroles and dark obsidian fields. Its raw basalt surface acts as a natural high-frequency baffle, dampening flutter echoes and accentuating low-end sub-frequencies.

Geological Base Black Dacite & Glassy Basalt
Reverberation (RT60) 4.8 Seconds (Caldera Crater)
Natural Resonator Active Subterranean Fumarole Vents
Pavilion Count 8 Site-Specific Works
Monumental Highlight • Crater Apex

Chamber of Fumaroles

Anne Holtrop & Toshiya Tsunoda

A massive circular monolith cast from molten volcanic aggregate directly over a dormant steam fissure. As sulfuric steam rises through internal labyrinthine acoustic chambers, it drives an aeolian organ that hums at the exact resonant frequency of the island's seismic tremor.

MATERIALS: Cast basalt slurry, carbonized oak, microphonic sensors.

Thirasia

The Western Rim • Cliffside Wind Harps

The rugged western boundary of the ancient prehistoric eruption. Thirasia rises 295 meters sheer from the Aegean Sea. The intense Cycladic winds crashing against the porous pumice cliffs produce constant aeolian harmonics that travel miles across the bay.

Geological Base Stratified White Pumice & Red Tuff
Reverberation (RT60) 6.2 Seconds (Cliff Caverns)
Natural Resonator 295m Vertical Pumice Shear Wall
Pavilion Count 7 Cliff Pavilions & Choral Steps
Cliffside Monument • Manolas Escarpment

Petrified Breath (Aeolian Cantata)

Smiljan Radić & Jana Winderen

Suspended over the abyss from titanium anchor cables, Radić’s translucent fiberglass boulders house 120-meter stretched piano wires that vibrate under the prevailing Meltemi wind. The resulting sound mimics human microtonal choir chants.

MATERIALS: Translucent resin boulders, tempered steel strings, wind-funnels.

Palea Kameni

The Thermal Rift • Hydrophone Thermal Baths

The oldest of the central volcanic islands, surrounded by mineral-rich geothermal hot springs that glow yellow with sulfur. Visitors enter buoyant acoustic immersion tanks submerged in 34°C water, listening to subaquatic soundscapes that travel through water five times faster than through air.

Geological Base Iron-Stained Hydrothermal Rhyolite
Reverberation (RT60) 8.5 Seconds (Underwater Caldarium)
Natural Resonator Pelagic Thermal Density Layers
Pavilion Count 6 Floating & Submerged Pods
Subaquatic Pavilion • St. Nicholas Cove

Pelagic Bathymetry

Forensic Architecture & Kali Malone

An underwater pipe organ tuned to ocean salinity. Submerged swimmers place their temples against bronze contact pads to perceive massive organ drone intervals through pure cranial bone conduction while immersed in volcanic mud.

MATERIALS: Marine bronze pipes, sulfur-resistant silicone, bone transducers.

Aspronisi

The White Crag • Anechoic Silence Sanctuary

Aspronisi ("White Island") is completely unpopulated, composed of brilliant white pumice and volcanic ash layers. The ultra-porous pumice absorbs almost 92% of incident acoustic energy, creating an open-air natural near-anechoic environment where footsteps are eerily swallowed.

Geological Base High-Porosity Compressed Ash & Chalk
Reverberation (RT60) 0.35 Seconds (Open-Air Anechoic Basin)
Natural Resonator Total Acoustic Absorption Wall
Pavilion Count 5 Silence Chambers & Optical Reflectors
Silence Sanctuary • Ash Plateau

Microtonal Pumice Organ

Ryoji Ikeda & Junya Ishigami

A featherweight pavilion constructed of ultra-thin chalk panels that funnel absolute quietude into a subterranean acoustic well. Inside, the visitor hears the internal circulating blood of their own ears tuned against ultra-precise sine waves.

MATERIALS: Ash-block lintels, stretched membrane prisms, sine frequency emitter.

Kolumbo Seamount

The Submerged Abyss • Telemetry Floating Vessel

Located 7 kilometers northeast of the caldera, Kolumbo is an active submarine volcano whose crater lies 505 meters below sea level. Since it cannot be physically walked upon, Biennial attendees board anchored research catamarans equipped with spatialized abyssal hydrophone arrays.

Sub-Pelagic Tremor Broadcasts

Assemble & Éliane Radigue Archive

Four subsea titanium hydrophones lowered into the bubbling throat of Kolumbo transmit boiling geothermal vents directly into an onboard wooden chamber designed to vibrate as a solitary acoustic instrument under starry nocturnal skies.

MATERIALS: Marine sonar relays, steamed chestnut vessel chamber, infrasonic actuators.

Archipelago Practicalities & FAQ

Crucial Expedition Guidelines

Footwear: High-traction ankle-support hiking boots are mandatory. The volcanic terrain consists of brittle basalt slag, sharp obsidian glass, and loose pumice gravel.

Solar Shuttles: Inter-island transfers depart every 30 minutes from Athinios Port and Fira Old Harbor. All boats are fully electric to minimize underwater acoustic pollution.

Acoustic Silence Zones: Cellular phones must remain in airplane mode on Aspronisi and inside the Nea Kameni caldera amphitheaters. Mobile loudspeakers are prohibited.

Ecological Sanction: The five islands are under strict UNESCO Geopark protection. Do not remove volcanic rocks, sulfur crystals, or disturb nesting pelagic shearwaters.

Your Biennial pass includes boarding passes for the official Archipelago Solar Catamaran fleet operating from Santorini (Athinios port and Fira harbor). Shuttles run continuously from 07:30 to 23:30 daily.
